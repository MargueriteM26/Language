BOY, 10, CONQUERS
THE CAPITAL'S FAMOUS TUBE

Young Adam completes his first solo journey on the London Underground,
changing trains twice — and never once looking back
By Our Junior Correspondent  ·  Published Wednesday, 30th July 2026

A ten-year-old boy from north London has become the talk of his household after successfully completing his first solo journey on the London Underground — one of the busiest and most complex rail networks in the world.

Adam, whose surname we have withheld to protect his privacy, set off from the bustlingBustling — full of busy, noisy activity concourse of King's Cross station armed with nothing more than an Oyster card, his mother's instructions, and a quiet determination to prove himself.

"He pressed his card on the reader and just walked straight through," his proud mother told this reporter. "I watched from the barrier and my heart was in my mouth — but he looked so calm."

Adam's route took him westbound on the Piccadilly line, before a change at Green Park and a final two stops on the Jubilee line to Westminster. On paper, it sounds straightforward. In practice, the Underground's labyrinthineLabyrinthine — extremely complicated, like a maze (labyrinth) corridors and dozens of exits can baffle even experienced adult travellers.

It was at Green Park that Adam faced his greatest test. Stepping off the Piccadilly line train, he found himself confronted by a long, dimly-lit corridor branching in several directions. "When in doubt, look up," his mother always told him. He looked up. A bold blue sign read: Jubilee Line →

The Jubilee line train that carried Adam to his final destination arrived at the platform with a great, cavernousCavernous — very large and hollow inside, like a huge cave (cavern) whoosh of warm air — the kind that only the deep tunnels of London's Underground can produce. Adam stepped on board, found a seat, and kept a careful eye on the route map above the doors.

Two stops later, he was there. Westminster. He walked calmly onto the platform, took the escalator up to street level, and emerged blinking into the grey London sky — the pale stone towers of the Palace of Westminster rising ahead of him like something from a history book.

His message to his mother was brief but triumphant: "I made it! Tube = brilliant."

By the time he slipped his earphones in and set off towards the River Thames, Adam had already earned the unspoken title that every true Londoner secretly covets: that of the seasoned commuterCommuter — someone who travels regularly, usually to school or work.

💬 Did You Know?

The London Underground — known as the Tube — is the oldest underground railway in the world. It opened in 1863 and today carries over 1 billion passengers a year on 11 different lines.

We asked Adam what advice he would give to other young travellers thinking of riding the Tube. His answer was short and very much his mother's: "Look up. The signs are always there."
