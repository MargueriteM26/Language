Adam and the Underground Adventure

A short story · approx. 450 words

Adam pressed his Oyster card against the yellow reader and heard the familiar beep. The heavy barriers swung open and he stepped onto the bustlingBustling – full of busy, noisy activity concourse of King's Cross station. He took a deep breath. Today was the day he would travel alone on the London Underground for the very first time.

His mum had given him strict instructions: take the Piccadilly line westbound, change at Green Park, then ride the Jubilee line two stops to Westminster. Simple enough — on paper. But the Underground felt anything but simple. Enormous maps covered the walls, each coloured line snakingSnaking – twisting and winding like a snake across the city like a tangle of wool.

Adam found the correct platform and squeezed onto a packed train. He navigatedNavigated – planned and found the right way to travel his way to a small gap near the doors, gripping a silver pole as the carriage swayed and rattled through the dark tunnel. The automated voice announced each station in a calm, clear tone: "The next station is Russell Square."

Three stops later, Green Park. Adam stepped onto the platform and paused. A long, labyrinthineLabyrinthine – extremely complicated, like a maze (labyrinth) corridor stretched ahead. Signs pointed left and right. For a moment, his stomach twisted with apprehensionApprehension – a feeling of worry or slight fear about what might happen. What if he took the wrong exit? What if he missed his stop?

He closed his eyes, took a slow breath, and thought about what his mum always said: "When in doubt, look up." He looked up. A bold blue sign read: Jubilee Line →. He followed it.

The Jubilee line train arrived with a cavernousCavernous – huge and hollow inside, like a great cave (cavern) whoosh of warm air. Adam stepped on, found a seat, and watched the map above the doors. One stop — Waterloo. Two stops — Westminster. He was ready. As the train pulled in, he stood, walked calmly to the doors, and stepped out onto the platform.

Outside, the pale stone towers of the Palace of Westminster rose against the grey sky. Adam grinned so widely his cheeks ached. He pulled out his phone and typed a message to his mum: "I made it! Tube = brilliant."

He pressed send, popped his earphones in, and set off towards the river — a seasoned commuterCommuter – someone who travels regularly, usually to school or work already.

Section B — Inference (read between the lines)
Inference

4. How do we know Adam was nervous at Green Park station? Use evidence from the text.

We know Adam was nervous because the text says "his stomach twisted with apprehension" — this physical reaction (twisting stomach) shows fear. He also "closed his eyes" and "took a slow breath", which are things people do to calm themselves down. The word apprehension itself means worry or mild fear.
Inference

5. Why do you think the author describes Adam as "a seasoned commuter already" at the end of the story? What does this tell us?

The word seasoned means experienced and confident. At the start Adam was nervous and uncertain. By the end, he walked calmly off the train and even sent a cheerful message to his mum. The author uses this phrase to show how much Adam has grown in confidence during the journey — even though it was only one trip, he feels like a pro.

Section C — Vocabulary in Context
Vocabulary

7. In the sentence "A long, labyrinthine corridor stretched ahead," what does labyrinthine suggest about the corridor?

Vocabulary

8. The author says the train arrived with a "cavernous whoosh of warm air." Why is the word cavernous a good choice here?

Cavernous means huge and hollow, like a cave. Underground tunnels are long, dark, and hollow — very cave-like. The word helps the reader picture the enormous rush of air that pushes through the tunnel as the train arrives, and reminds us of just how big and deep the Underground really is.
